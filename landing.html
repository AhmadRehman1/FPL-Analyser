Fantasy Premier League · Decision support

Weekly FPL decisions,
backed by model evidence.

FPL Quant reads your real Fantasy Premier League squad and turns it into clear transfer, captain and chip calls each gameweek — with a dated audit trail and data provenance behind every recommendation. No invented accuracy scores.

Open the app › View model track record

What it gives you

A second opinion on the three decisions that decide most FPL gameweeks — built from your actual squad, not a generic template.

Transfer calls
Hold vs. use your free transfer, ranked player swaps, and the horizon value of each — per gameweek.
Captain & chips
Who gets the armband this week, and whether Wildcard, Free Hit, Triple Captain or Bench Boost is worth playing now.
Future planner
See your squad and projected points across upcoming gameweeks, and try a hypothetical transfer before you commit.
Live tracking
Rank movement and live points during matches, plus price-watch and injury news surfaced when they matter.

Add your team in a minute

No account to create here — it runs on free infrastructure you already have.

1
Open the app
Tap “Open the app” above. It loads instantly as a installable web app — no install required to try it.
2
Add your FPL team ID
From your team’s URL on fantasy.premierleague.com. This opens a GitHub issue that a workflow processes automatically — usually ready in 1–2 minutes, no approval needed.
3
Get your weekly plan
Your real squad’s transfer, captain and chip recommendations refresh on a schedule. Everything is read-only — nothing is ever submitted to FPL for you.

Trust, not marketing

Every recommendation links to the evidence behind it — and there’s a public page for the model’s whole history.

Public track record
A dated, per-gameweek audit trail of what the model recommended — plus data provenance for every run. See it ›
Honest about limits
The model is v1 — not yet recalibrated or walk-forward backtested. The track record page says so plainly rather than hiding it.
ⓘ
What this is, and isn’t. FPL Quant is decision support, not a promise of points. The model is early-stage and openly not yet backtested — treat its calls as a transparent second opinion alongside your own judgement.
